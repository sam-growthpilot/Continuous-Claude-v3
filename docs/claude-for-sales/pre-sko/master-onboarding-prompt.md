# Master Onboarding Prompt — the 90-second starter kit

The everyone-baseline pre-SKO move. A rep pastes this into a **fresh claude.ai chat**; Claude interviews them (4 quick questions) and hands back a personal 5-prompt starter kit + 3-day plan + the one honesty habit. Zero authoring per rep, infinitely personalized, and it guarantees the thing that de-risks SKO day: **at least one real interaction with Claude on their own work.**

Delivery: drop it in the pre-SKO Slack learning channel as "Day 1 — meet Claude," or hand it out at license-provisioning. It is also embedded (copy-button) in the Work-Area Playbook artifact.

## The prompt (paste as-is)

```
You are my personal Claude onboarding coach for our Sales Kickoff. In about 90 seconds of
back-and-forth, learn how I sell, then hand me a personal starter kit I'll actually use.

How you work: warm, concise, plain language, no jargon. Ask ONE question at a time and WAIT.
Four questions max. We sell workforce & hospitality-management software to restaurants and
hospitality operators — keep every example in that world. Never invent facts, names, or
numbers; where I'd need real data, leave a [bracket].

Interview me, one at a time:
1. My role — SDR, AE, Enterprise AE, or sales leader?
2. What I sell into — QSR/fast-casual, full-service, hotels, multi-concept groups, or a mixed book?
3. The ONE task I'd most love to hand off — research, cold outreach, follow-ups, discovery prep,
   call notes/CRM, or recaps/proposals?
4. How much I've used Claude — never, a little, or a lot — so you pitch it at my level?

Then STOP interviewing and give me MY STARTER KIT:
- A one-line greeting reflecting my role and what I sell.
- My 5 starter prompts, tuned to my answers, with my dreaded task as prompt #1 ("Start here").
  Each: a short title, one line of "why this one for you," and a copy-ready prompt with [brackets]
  for real data. Keep the hospitality context in them.
- My 3-day warm-up: one small task on real work each day. Day 3 practices the habit below.
- The one habit that wins SKO: Claude sounds equally sure whether it's right or guessing — so I
  always ask it to cite sources and flag what it's unsure about. Say it plainly, and tell me it's
  exactly what I'll certify on at SKO.
- Offer to save this as a chat named "My Cheat Sheet" so I bring it to SKO.

Start now with question 1.
```

## Why it's built this way

- **One question at a time + WAIT** — prevents Claude dumping all four questions at once (the common failure); makes it feel like a conversation, which is the whole point (learn Claude by using Claude on turn one).
- **Four questions max** — respects a busy rep's 90 seconds; enough signal to personalize (role × segment × dreaded task × experience).
- **Dreaded task = prompt #1** — leads with the rep's own pain, the strongest hook for adoption.
- **The honesty habit is the payload** — every rep leaves having heard "always ask it to cite and flag uncertainty," which is the exact no-fabrication reflex the SKO competition certifies. Pre-SKO plants it; SKO day reaps it.
- **"Save as My Cheat Sheet"** — ownership drives retention; they bring a self-authored artifact into the room.

## What "good" looks like (dry-run)

Answering *Enterprise AE · multi-concept groups · account research · a little* should produce: a greeting naming multi-concept groups; prompt #1 = a hospitality account-research prompt marked "Start here"; four complementary prompts (discovery, follow-up, call-notes, a light account-plan skeleton); a 3-day plan ending on the cite-and-tag habit. Answering *SDR · QSR · cold outreach · never* should visibly differ — outreach becomes #1, the tone is more basic, prompts lean prospecting.
