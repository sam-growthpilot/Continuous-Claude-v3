The infrastructure behind the intelligence

Build a Claude that knows your accounts.

Out of the box, Claude is brilliant but generic — it forgets you between chats. A Project turns it into a specialist that knows your role, holds your reference material, and runs your repeatable moves the same way every time. This is how you build one before SKO — the work area, not just the conversation.

§1 The shift

A chat is disposable. A work area compounds.

Every time you open a blank chat, you start from zero — re-explaining who you are, re-pasting your battlecard, hoping for consistency. A Project fixes all three at once: it remembers your brief, holds your documents, and runs your skills. You set it up once; every chat inside it starts smart.

Blank chat

Start from scratch, every time

  • Re-explain your role and what you sell
  • Re-paste the battlecard, the ICP, the last plan
  • Different output shape every time
  • Nothing carries to the next chat
A Project

Start smart, every time

  • Claude already knows your role & rules
  • Your reference library is loaded and searchable
  • Skills produce the same clean output on demand
  • Every chat in the project inherits all of it
§2 Anatomy of a Project

Four components. Tap each one.

A Project is just these four parts working together. The first three are for everyone; the fourth is the advanced move once you have a few skills.

§3 The insight that makes Docs powerful

Upload once. Don't paste every time.

Claude has a working memory (the "context window") — the space it thinks in during one chat. Paste a big battlecard into every message and you fill that space with reference material, leaving less room to actually reason. Project Docs solve this: upload your files once, and Claude searches them only when relevant — like a library it checks, not a stack it holds in its lap.

§4 The build-along playbook

Build your first work area — in order.

Seven steps, foundations first. Each step opens to a click-path and a copy-paste starter prompt. Don't write anything cold — you'll have Claude build most of this with you.

0Lay the foundation firstGlobal instructions + a 'Main' assistant project▸

Before any project, tell Claude who you are once, at the account level — your global instructions. Then build a 'Main' project that acts as your everyday assistant and knows how you work. Every specialist project you build later inherits a Claude that already gets you. This one move makes everything downstream sharper.

Where →Settings → your personal/custom instructions (global). Then create a Project called "[Your Name] — Assistant."
Paste → Claude drafts your global instructions
Help me write my personal Claude instructions. Interview me briefly, one question at a time: my name, my sales role, what I sell and to whom, how I like you to respond (tone, length, format), and any standing rules I always want followed. Then draft a tight set of custom instructions — under 200 words — that I can paste into my account settings so you understand me in every chat.
1Create a targeted work areaOne project, one job▸

Make a Project for one job — "Enterprise Account Research," "Discovery Prep," "Follow-Ups." Narrow beats broad: a focused project gives sharper, more consistent results than a catch-all. You'll have several small projects, not one giant one.

Where →Projects → New Project → name it for the job, not the tool.
2Chat to build your Custom InstructionsThe brief that spans the whole project▸

The Custom Instructions are the project's standing brief — they apply to every chat inside it. Don't write them cold. Paste the prompt below and let Claude interview you and draft them; review, tweak, and save into the project's Instructions.

Paste inside your new project
I'm setting up this Claude Project for [purpose — e.g. Enterprise account research on hospitality prospects]. Interview me one question at a time to understand: what this project is for, my role and what I sell, the output I want, the sources of truth I'll add as docs, and my rules (always cite sources, tag confidence, never fabricate a name or number). After 4–5 questions, draft this project's Custom Instructions — a clear standing brief that governs every chat here. Then wait for my edits before we finalize.
3Load your Project DocsYour reference library — searched, not pasted▸

Upload your reference set to the project's knowledge: your product battlecard, your ICP, a strong past account plan, a discovery framework. This becomes the project's searchable library (see §3) — it grounds every chat in Fourth's real positioning, and it doesn't eat your working memory. Upload once; benefit in every chat.

Where →Inside the project → add files to the project's knowledge / docs.
4Pick a repeated task to turn into a SkillFind your first "I do this every week"▸

Look for one move you do the same way over and over — "research an account," "turn call notes into a follow-up," "prep discovery." That repeatable move is your first Skill: package the how once so you never re-prompt it. Pick the one that eats the most time.

5Build the Skill with Claudeskill-forge interviews you and co-writes it▸

Use the skill-builder helper — skill-forge (Dave is publishing it to your workspace). You describe the task; it interviews you one question at a time — inputs, exact steps, output shape, rules — then drafts the skill and shows it to you before saving. You don't write a skill from a blank page; you answer questions.

Paste → kick off the skill build
Use the skill-forge skill to help me turn a repeated piece of my sales work into a reusable Skill. The task: [describe it — e.g. "research a hospitality prospect and produce a confidence-tagged 5-card brief"]. Interview me one question at a time about: what kicks it off, the inputs, the exact steps you should follow, the output shape I want, and the hard rules (cite a source for every fact, tag confidence, never fabricate a name — role-only + "verify on LinkedIn" when a name isn't public). Then draft the SKILL.md and show it to me before we finalize.

CLAUDEGot it. What are the inputs — just a company name, or also segment and territory?

Then wire it in. Add one line to the project's Custom Instructions so Claude knows when to reach for it:

Add to your Custom Instructions
When I ask you to research an account, use the account-research skill and follow its output shape exactly — every fact confidence-tagged, no fabricated names.
6Let Claude suggest the next layerOrchestrator + keep honing▸

A great work area is grown, not built once. Ask the project what to add next. If you've built a few skills or a multi-step process, Claude will suggest an orchestrator skill — a conductor that runs them in sequence with checkpoints (research → synthesize → draft → you approve). That's exactly how the SKO Account Cockpit works: a planner that sequences research and plan-building, pausing at each gate.

Paste → let Claude audit and suggest
Review this project — my Custom Instructions, my docs, and the skills I've added. Tell me: what's missing, what would make it sharper, and whether my skills or process are complex enough to deserve an orchestrator skill that runs them in sequence with checkpoints. If yes, sketch what that orchestrator would do, step by step, and where it should pause for my approval.
§5 Bake in the one rule
◆ The honesty guardrail

Put this in every project's Custom Instructions.

Claude sounds equally confident whether it's right or guessing. The fix isn't to remember to check every time — it's to build the check into your infrastructure so it's automatic:

• Cite a source (a link) for every fact.
• Tag confidence on each claim: [VERIFIED] / [REPORTED] / [INFERRED].
• Never invent a name, title, or number — write "not found in public sources — verify manually" instead.
• When a person's name isn't public, give the role only + "verify on LinkedIn."
Set it once here and it runs on every chat in the project. This is the exact reflex you'll certify on at SKO — so by the day itself, it's already a habit, not a lesson.
§6 Your very first move

Not sure where to start? Meet Claude first.

Before you build a project, run the 90-second onboarding: paste the prompt below into a fresh chat and Claude will interview you and hand you a personal 5-prompt starter kit. It's the gentlest on-ramp — then come back and build your first work area.

Paste into a new claude.ai chat
You are my personal Claude onboarding coach for our Sales Kickoff. In about 90 seconds of back-and-forth, learn how I sell, then hand me a personal starter kit I'll actually use. How you work: warm, concise, plain language, no jargon. Ask ONE question at a time and WAIT. Four questions max. We sell workforce & hospitality-management software to restaurants and hospitality operators — keep every example in that world. Never invent facts, names, or numbers; where I'd need real data, leave a [bracket]. Interview me, one at a time: (1) my role — SDR, AE, Enterprise AE, or sales leader? (2) what I sell into — QSR/fast-casual, full-service, hotels, multi-concept groups, or a mixed book? (3) the ONE task I'd most love to hand off — research, cold outreach, follow-ups, discovery prep, call notes/CRM, or recaps/proposals? (4) how much I've used Claude — never, a little, or a lot? Then STOP and give me MY STARTER KIT: a one-line greeting reflecting my role and what I sell; my 5 starter prompts tuned to my answers, with my dreaded task as prompt #1 ("Start here"), each with a short title, one line of "why this one for you," and a copy-ready prompt with [brackets] for real data; a 3-day warm-up on real work (Day 3 practices the habit); and the one habit that wins SKO — always ask me to cite sources and flag what you're unsure about, because you sound equally sure when you're guessing. Offer to save it as a chat named "My Cheat Sheet." Start now with question 1.
